# Day 4 - 2026-10-16 (Friday)

**Class:** Team warm-up with my first jump rope round; kicks on the heavy bag at three heights; 4-person station rotation (tire jumps, plank, padwork with the coach, rest); one-twos for volume and power; partner drills for checking and catching kicks, then catch-and-sweep chains.

## Lesson

### Jump rope
**From class**
- First time jumping rope. 3 minutes.
- The rope is heavy, so you need hand strength to turn it. My hands got tired after the first 100 jumps.
- After 100 jumps I had to stop. Then I rested a few seconds and kept going, stopping and starting again at a slower pace until the 3 minutes were up.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- With a heavy rope, the turn comes from the **wrists and forearms**, not from swinging the whole arm. Keep the elbows close to your sides and the hands around hip height.
- **Jump low** - just high enough for the rope to pass, about 2-3 cm. Land softly on the balls of the feet, heels never touching the floor. Big jumps burn energy fast.
- Slower but continuous beats fast bursts with long stops. A steady, easy bounce is the target for a 3-minute round, the same length as a fight round.
- When you're out of breath, don't stand still - bounce on the spot without the rope and swing it at your side, then go back in.
- Breathe through the nose as long as you can, and relax the shoulders. Tight shoulders tire the arms faster.
- Home (no rope needed): 3 x 1 minute of rope-less skipping - small bounces on the balls of the feet, wrists making small circles.

### Kicks on the heavy bag - three heights
**From class**
- **Low kick**, left and right: 15 each.
- **Middle kick** (body kick), left and right: 15 each.
- **High kick** (head kick), left and right: 15 each.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Same roundhouse kick from Day 3 at three heights: low (calf / thigh), middle (ribs), high (head). The left kicks are switch kicks.
- On every kick: step out at an angle, pivot on the ball of the standing foot, turn the hips over, land with the shin.
- On the bag, let the kick swing through the target instead of stopping at the surface - that's where the power comes from.
- Count quality, not just reps: if the last 5 high kicks fall apart, kick them lower with good form instead of losing balance.
- Hands up between kicks. Beginners often drop the guard after a kick to reset their feet.

### Station rotation
**From class**
- Groups of 4 people rotate through the stations [tuần tự]:
  1. Jumping onto the truck tire.
  2. Plank.
  3. Padwork with the coach: strike on the coach's signal - combos, punches and kicks.
  4. Rest.
- I did 5 sets.
- After that: 100 one-twos, then 10 **power one-twos** - hitting as hard as I can.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- **Striking on the coach's signal** trains reaction: see or hear the call, throw, return to guard. Hit and get the hands back - don't stay extended waiting for the next call.
- **Plank:** elbows under shoulders, body in one straight line, ribs down and glutes tight. If the hips sag, the lower back takes the load instead of the core.
- **Tire jumps:** land softly with knees bent, on the balls of the feet. Same springy feet as the jump rope.
- **Power one-twos:** power comes from the legs and the hip turn, not the arm. Pivot the rear foot hard on the cross and breathe out sharply. Keep the hand loose until impact, then tight.
- Full power still has form: the jab still comes back as the cross goes out, and both hands return to guard. Beginners often swing wide or lean forward when they try to hit hard.

### Checking, catching and sweeping kicks
**From class**
- Partner drills, like light sparring, on defending kicks, in this order:
  1. **Check** the kick [be] on both sides when the partner kicks.
  2. **Catch** the partner's kick [hold their leg], step back, **release the leg** [throw the leg], then kick.
  3. Chain drill: A kicks B, B catches it, steps back, releases and kicks -> then A catches B's kick, steps back and kicks B.
  4. The kick we learned for this is a kick to the **ankle**, while the hand pushes the opponent's head or collar at the same time to make them fall. This is a **sweep**.
- Done on both left and right.
- **Coach's tip:** if you catch the leg with your left hand, step back with your left leg, then kick. If you catch with your right hand, step back with your right leg, then kick.
- **Catching their left kick:** my left hand stays up in guard, my right hand catches the leg. I step my right leg back and pull their leg to my side, then step the right leg forward again to become the **standing leg** [chân trụ]. Then I sweep their ankle with my left leg while my left hand pushes their head or collarbone.
- **Catching their right kick:** my left hand catches the leg. I step my left leg back and pull their leg towards me at the same time, then step the left leg forward again to become the **standing leg** [chân trụ]. Then I sweep their ankle with my right leg while my right hand pushes their face or collarbone.
- One hand is still holding the leg, so the other (free) hand does the push.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- This sequence is usually called **catch and sweep** (also "catch and dump"). The opponent is on one leg after you catch the kick, so a sweep to the standing leg plus a push up top takes away their balance.
- **The catch:** scoop the kicking leg with your arm and trap it against your ribs, close to your body. Catching with the hand far out in front gives the opponent room to pull the leg back or hit you.
- **The step back** drags the caught leg with you, stretching the opponent out and putting more weight onto their standing leg - that's what makes the sweep work.
- **The sweep:** kick low and sweep through the ankle of their **standing** leg, shin or foot cutting in a low arc. It's a sweep, not a hard strike - you're taking the foot away, not trying to hurt the ankle.
- **The push:** push the head or collar in the opposite direction to the sweep at the same moment. Leg goes one way, upper body goes the other.
- **The pattern in one line:** catching hand and stepping leg are the same side; sweeping leg and pushing hand are the other side. Catch right, step right - sweep left, push left.
- **Four beats, both sides:** catch - step back and pull - step forward to plant the standing leg - sweep and push together.
- Home: walk through it slowly in the air, 5 times each side, saying it out loud: "catch right, step back, step forward, sweep left, push left" - then the mirror version.
