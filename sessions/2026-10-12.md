# Day 2 - 2026-10-12 (Monday)

**Class:** Conditioning-heavy class - punch review, tire jumps, agility ladder circuits, station rotation (pads, heavy bag, first sparring), plank relay with one-twos, plank finisher.

## Lesson

### Warm-up and review of the 6 punches
**From class**
- Warm-up, then a review of all 6 punches (1-6).

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Use the review to check one cue per punch, not everything at once: jab - shoulder rolls up; cross - pivot the rear foot; hooks - fist, elbow and shoulder on one level; uppercuts - forearm vertical, small dip.
- Every punch returns to guard along the same line it went out.

### Staying relaxed
**From class**
- Feedback I keep getting: I'm too stiff - my hands, my guard, my shoulders and my neck. The tension makes my muscles tire faster.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Tension burns energy all the time, even when you're not punching. A relaxed fighter only tightens for a split second on impact, the same loose-to-tight rule as the fist on Day 1.
- Shoulders down, away from your ears. Tuck the chin by lowering it, not by shrugging the shoulders up to meet it - that hunched shrug is what tires the neck.
- Guard: gloves rest lightly against your face, elbows close to the ribs, hands open-ish inside the gloves. Firm contact, not squeezing.
- Breathe out on every strike. Holding your breath makes the whole upper body lock up.
- Home drill: shadowbox for 2 minutes at half speed, saying "shoulders down" to yourself. Between rounds, shake out the arms and roll the shoulders.

### Tire jumps
**From class**
- Bounce on a big car tire for 3 minutes, in fighting stance. My calves were burning but recovered fast.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Builds calf endurance and springy ankles, the light bouncing rhythm Muay Thai fighters move with.
- Small bounces on the balls of the feet, knees soft, guard up, stance shape kept the whole time.
- Breathe steadily; don't hold your breath to get through it.
- Home: skipping rope, or small bounces in stance on the floor, 2 minutes.

### Agility ladder circuit 1
**From class**
- Hop through the agility ladder on one foot - left foot, then right foot.
- Then 5 frog jumps (nhảy cóc), then 5 squat jumps.
- 5 sets planned - I did 4.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Trains quick feet and explosive legs, which later power your knees and kicks.
- Land softly on the balls of the feet, knees pointing the same way as your toes.
- Swing the arms on jumps to help drive up.
- When tired, slow down and keep the form clean rather than rushing sloppy reps.

### Agility ladder circuit 2
**From class**
- In-and-out squat jumps: both feet into a square, then both feet out to the sides of the ladder, landing in a squat. 5 times.
- Then a duck walk - moving forward in a deep squat on the balls of the feet.
- 5 sets planned - I did 1.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The duck walk builds hip mobility and leg endurance, and it's one of the hardest drills for beginners.
- Keep the chest up and take short steps; the heels can stay lifted.
- If your knees hurt (not just burn), stop and tell the coach.
- Wide, soft landing on the "out" jumps, chest up, then explode back in.

### Station rotation: pads, heavy bag, sparring
**From class**
- The group rotates between three stations: padwork with the coach, the heavy bag, and sparring (two people at a time).
- My rotation: pads with the coach -> sparring with a partner -> back to the bag.
- The bag is heavy.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The heavy bag barely moves, so it shows you whether the power comes from your legs and hips. Turn the hip and pivot - don't push with the arm.
- Aim to hit with the first two knuckles and a straight wrist; the bag punishes a bent wrist.
- On pads, react to what the coach calls and return to guard after each combo, even when tired.

### Sparring (first time)
**From class**
- First sparring. I mostly defended and moved with footwork.
- My partners' punches landed hard on my guard, and my own gloves were pushed back into my face.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Your glove hitting your own face is called **getting hit through your guard**. It happens when the gloves float in front of the face. Press them lightly against the forehead or cheekbones, elbows tight, chin tucked, so guard and head move as one.
- Your instinct is right: better than taking the punch is getting off the line. Step back out of range, **slip** (a small head move to the side), or **parry** (a small tap that redirects the punch).
- A simple beginner plan: jab when they step into range, then step out. Or one-two, then move off at an angle. Good moments to attack are right after they finish a combo, or when their hands drop.
- In sparring, power isn't the goal. Timing and accuracy come first; power comes from legs and hips later.
- It's normal to ask a stronger partner to go light, or to ask the coach for technical sparring while you're new. -> **Ask the coach**.
- Protective gear (mouthguard, headgear) differs by gym. -> **Ask the coach** what to bring.

### Plank relay with one-twos
**From class**
- Everyone holds a plank while one person hits 100 one-twos on the coach's pads, then the next person goes. My turn: 50.
- 2 rounds, 4 people.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- One-two = jab then cross. The jab comes back as the cross goes out, so one hand always guards.
- Keep pivoting on the cross even when tired, and exhale on each punch.
- Plank: elbows under shoulders, glutes squeezed. Drop to your knees before your lower back sags.

### Power one-twos
**From class**
- Finish with 10 power one-twos.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Power comes from turning the hips and fully pivoting the rear foot on the cross, with a slight sit-down into the punch.
- Still snap back to guard after each one - power shots leave you most open.

### Plank finisher
**From class**
- Last set before ending: plank 1 min 30 s, rest 30 s, plank 1 min 30 s.
- I only gave up 5 seconds before the end.
- Then 10 crunches and 10 push-ups.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Compared with Day 1 (a 2-minute plank where I dropped 3 times), this is 3 minutes of plank with only the last 5 seconds missed - after a much harder class.
- In the last 30 seconds, breathe out slowly and squeeze the glutes; shaking is normal and doesn't mean you have to stop.
- If the lower back starts to sag, drop to your knees for a few seconds, then come back up rather than stopping completely.

## Conditioning
- Tire jumps: 3 minutes.
- 10 knee push-ups, 10 crunches, 10 squats.
- Agility ladder circuit 1: 4 of 5 sets.
- Agility ladder circuit 2: 1 of 5 sets.
- Plank relay: 2 rounds, 50 one-twos on my turn.
- 10 power one-twos.
- Plank finisher: 1:30 plank, 30 s rest, 1:30 plank - gave up 5 seconds before the end.
- 10 crunches, 10 push-ups.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Conditioning improves fast in the first few weeks. Finishing more sets each class is the real progress marker.
- Pace yourself: go steady in set 1 instead of all-out, and breathe out on every effort.

## Body
- Calves burned on the tire, then recovered quickly.
- Heart rate very high in the conditioning sets - it felt like my heart couldn't keep up.
- Shoulders extremely tired - I could barely lift my arms afterwards.

**Common causes in beginners** *(added by Pi Liang - not a diagnosis)*
- Shoulder fatigue: holding the guard up for a whole class, plus hundreds of punches and push-ups. Being too tense (see "Staying relaxed") makes it much worse.
- Getting out of breath early is normal in the first weeks; your conditioning adapts quickly.
- Stop and tell your coach if you ever feel chest pain or dizziness, or your heartbeat doesn't settle after resting - and see a doctor if it happens.
