## Lesson

### Kicks on the heavy bag - low, body, high
**From class**
- 10 kicks at each height - **low kick**, **body kick** [mid], **high kick** - with the left leg and the right leg.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Same three heights as Day 9: step the standing foot out off the line, lean to the side, turn the hips over, land with the shin.
- Lean only as much as the height needs - a little for the low kick, most for the high kick.
- 10 per height is few enough to make every kick a full one: finish the hip turn on each, even when slow.
- Home: Hip-turn kick drill and Low base and balance builder (drills.md).

### Plum + step-back knee
**From class**
- Grab the opponent's neck with both hands, **right hand on top of the left hand** - the **plum** (double collar tie).
- Pull their head onto my shoulder, so it covers their view.
- Pulled onto my **right shoulder** -> step the **right leg back**, drag them towards me, then **right knee**.
- Pulled onto my **left shoulder** -> step the **left leg back**, drag them towards me, then **left knee**.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Hands stacked on the back of the head (not on the neck, fingers not interlocked), elbows squeezed together in front of their collarbone. The elbows inside are what stop them from posting up.
- Pulling the head down onto your shoulder **breaks their posture**: bent forward with the head trapped, they can't see the knee coming or stand up straight to defend it.
- The step back moves your body away from them, so their head and weight fall towards you and onto the knee. The pull comes from the step and your body weight, not just the arms.
- Same side: head on the right shoulder, right foot back, right knee. The leg that stepped back is already loaded for the knee.
- Common mistakes: pulling only with the arms (you tire fast and a strong neck won't move), and stepping back without pulling - then they just stand up.
- Home: Clinch pull and swim (drills.md), round 1.

### Stopping the clinch entry + turn and knee
**From class**
- When the opponent raises their hands to grab my neck: put both hands on their arms, just **above the elbows**, and push both arms down.
- My arms must be **inside** their arms.
- Then quickly grab their neck with one hand.
- Head onto my **right shoulder** -> step the **right leg** out to the right at **45 degrees** (not straight to the side), then hold the head and turn fast, so the **left leg** ends up behind the right leg. Then **left knee**.
- Head onto my **left shoulder** -> the mirror of it, then **right knee**.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Hands above the elbows control their arms where they're weakest - they can't reach the neck if the elbows are pinned down.
- Arms inside theirs is **inside position**: whoever has their arms inside controls the head. Outside arms can only reach around.
- The grab after the push is a **single collar tie** (Day 9) - the hand cups the back of the head, forearm in front of their collarbone.
- The step and turn is a **clinch turn**: you pivot, they get dragged around in a circle and lose their balance - the knee lands while they're trying to get their feet back under them.
- Turn with the whole body (legs and hips), not the arm. The arm just holds the head on your shoulder.
- The knee is the opposite side to the shoulder: after the turn, that leg is the one behind, so it has the room and the load to drive up.

### Pummeling - fighting for inside position
**From class**
- When I push their arms down, the opponent can do the same: wrap around my arms and push them down. Then I do it again.
- We keep going until one of us is faster and grabs the other's neck.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- This is **pummeling**: both people "swim" their arms back inside, one arm at a time, to win inside position.
- Swim one arm at a time and keep the other one in - if both arms go out together, they get both arms inside.
- Swim close to your own body (the hand slides in under their arm, past your own chest), not in a big circle - shorter path, faster, less tiring.
- Keep the head up and close, and stay on your feet - most beginners lean in and push with tense arms, which is why the arms burn out.
- Home: Clinch pull and swim (drills.md), round 2.

### Breaking the clinch - push the face
**From class**
- When the opponent grabs my neck and pulls my head onto their shoulder, I can't see, but I can still feel where their face and head are.
- Use my hand to push their face away and break the grab.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The palm on their face pushes their head back, and where the head goes, the body follows - they can't hold your neck with their head turned away.
- Push the face away and to the side while you bring your own head up, so your posture comes back as theirs breaks.
- Their arms are full holding your neck - your free hand finds their face by feel, sliding up from their chest or shoulder.
- After the break, strike or get out straight away - if you stand there, they grab again.
