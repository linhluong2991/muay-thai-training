## Lesson

### Kicks on the heavy bag - four targets, both legs
**From class**
- Same four targets as last session: shin, thigh, body, head.
- 15 kicks at each target, right leg and left leg.
- On the right kick I hit the bag with my **little toe** [pinky toe] - it's now blistered, purple and very painful (see Body).

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The toe hits first when the **foot lands instead of the shin** - usually because you kick from too far away, so the foot reaches the bag before the shin does.
- Fix the distance before the kick: if the foot keeps landing, take one small step in, then kick.
- Keep the foot pointed and in line with the shin, so the toes don't curl or catch the bag on the way through.
- Pivot fully on the standing foot - if the hips don't turn over, the leg swings in flat and the foot leads.
- Home: Shin, not foot - bag distance drill (drills.md). While the toe hurts, do it barefoot and very slow, touching a pillow, not a wall.

### Straight knee - rear (right)
**From class**
- Like the left knee (below), but without the hop - knee straight from stance.
- Pull the **heel tight to the thigh** [bắp chân sát vào đùi - calf against the thigh], **point the toes** [mũi chân duỗi ra], so the knee becomes the tip of an arrow.
- **Drive the hips forward** to push the knee into the target.
- Same-side hand pulls down to add momentum. The other hand guards the head.
- Return to stance and guard after the knee.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The power comes from the hips, not from lifting the leg: rise onto the ball of the standing foot and push the hips through, so the knee travels **forward** into the target, not just up.
- Stay tall: lean the shoulders back slightly so the hips can move forward - but keep the chin tucked and eyes on the target.
- Common beginner mistakes: lifting the knee straight up without the hip drive, bending forward at the waist, and dropping both hands.
- The arm swing: the same-side arm swings down and back past the hip as the knee comes up, which helps the hip drive. The other glove stays on your face.
- Home: Guard-up knee and teep drill (drills.md).

### Switch knee (left)
**From class**
- **Switch** first: hop to bring the right foot forward [jump the right feet forward]. The left foot lands behind on the ball of the foot, ready to drive.
- Then the knee: heel tight to the thigh, toes pointed, knee like an arrow, **drive the hips forward**.
- Left hand pulls down to add momentum.
- **Return to stance** [back to defense position] - two ways:
  - **Left foot lands in front** of the right foot: push back with the left hand, then both feet step back with the backward footwork I learned before.
  - **Left foot lands behind** the right foot: pull the right foot back, then both feet step back once more.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The switch is the same hop as the switch kick (Day 6): small, feet low, and knee straight off it in one beat - no landing and stopping.
- The switch puts the left leg at the back, so the left knee has more distance to build speed - that's why it can feel stronger than a lead knee without the switch.
- After any knee you're close to the opponent and briefly on one leg - getting back to stance fast is part of the technique, which is why the coach taught you the return.
- Common beginner mistake: the hop is too big and the hands drop during it. Keep the guard on the face through the switch.
- Home: Guard-up knee and teep drill (drills.md), round 2.

### Lead teep (left)
**From class**
- Push kick with the front (left) leg.
- Make contact with the **ball of the foot**.
- Left hand pulls down to add momentum.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Chamber first: knee up towards the chest, then extend the leg straight out and push the hips forward into the target.
- Pull the toes back so the ball of the foot lands - pointed toes can jam.
- The teep is a push, not a slap: aim through the target (the belly or hips), then bring the foot back to stance fast.
- Common beginner mistakes: leaning back too far (easy to push over), kicking up instead of straight out, and dropping the guard.
- Home: Guard-up knee and teep drill (drills.md), round 3.

### Rear teep (right)
**From class**
- Like the left teep, but land with the **whole foot** [the whole feet - flat sole].
- Right hand pulls down to add momentum.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The rear teep travels further, so it carries more push - the flat sole spreads that push over a bigger area.
- Same chamber: knee up, then drive straight out with the hips; the standing foot rises onto the ball and turns slightly out.
- Bring the foot back to stance after the push - don't let it drop forward and leave you square.
- Common beginner mistake: the kick goes up and out with no hip, so it taps instead of pushes.
- While the right little toe hurts, skip rear teeps at home until it's checked.

### Key points for knees and teeps
**From class**
- When I knee or teep, both my hands tend to drop to create force. **Coach's correction:** keep the hand up guarding the head.
- I have to **drive the hips forward** on every knee and teep. This is very hard - my hip muscles feel tight [căng] and my back feels stiff, so it's hard to push forward.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Only the same-side arm swings; the other glove stays on your face. If both hands drop, your head is open while you're on one leg.
- Hip drive is a new movement pattern - tightness at the front of the hips is common at the start, especially if you sit a lot.
- Think "belt buckle to the target": tilt the pelvis forward, rather than arching the lower back. Arching the back is a common reason it feels stiff.
- Slow reps first. Speed and power come once the hip drive feels natural.
- Home: Guard-up knee and teep drill (drills.md), plus the hip flexor lunge at the end.
