## Lesson

### Stance
**From class**
- Left foot forward. Rear (right) foot turned out about 45 degrees.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Feet about shoulder-width apart. Don't stand on one line like a tightrope, or you'll tip over sideways.
- Knees slightly soft, not locked.
- A Muay Thai stance is usually more upright than a boxing stance, with a bit more weight on the back leg. That keeps the front leg light, so later you can lift it to block kicks or throw a teep.
- Home drill: stand in stance in front of a mirror for 1 minute. Check your foot angle and width, then shake it out and do it again.

### Footwork - forward, back, left, right
**From class**
- Move in all four directions.
- Keep about one arm's length between me and the opponent.
- Move on the balls of the feet (forefoot), with the back heel slightly raised.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Weight on the balls of the feet keeps you light and ready to move or pivot. Flat feet make you slow and easy to push off balance.
- Back heel only slightly up, not tiptoe. Raising it too high makes you bouncy and unstable.
- Step-drag rule: the foot nearest the direction you're going moves first, and the other foot follows the same distance. Forward = front foot first. Back = back foot first. Left = left foot first. Right = right foot first.
- Never cross your feet, and never bring them together.
- Small steps. Your stance should look the same after every step.
- Keep your guard up while you move. Beginners often drop their hands when they focus on their feet.
- One arm's length is your **range**: close enough that your jab reaches the opponent.

### Guard and defence basics
**From class**
- Both hands up covering the face, around the eyes.
- Chin down - hide the chin.
- Eyes on the opponent's **base of the neck** - the dip between the collarbones. *(Confirmed with my coach on Day 2.)*

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Fists at eyebrow or cheekbone height, elbows tucked in to protect the ribs.
- Chin tucked, and shoulders slightly raised to help cover it.
- Why the base of the neck: it's the centre of the body, so your side vision catches both hands *and* both legs at once. Watching the gloves makes you chase feints; watching the feet makes you miss punches. In Muay Thai, kicks and knees come from below, so this wide view matters.

### 1 - Jab (lead straight, left hand)
**From class**
- Small step forward with the left foot as you punch.
- Arm goes fully straight. The fist ends in line with the top of the nose (centreline), with the eyes looking along the fist.
- Left shoulder rolls forward.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The rolled-up shoulder also covers your chin while the arm is out.
- Turn the fist over as it lands, but keep a slight tilt rather than fully palm-down. *(Coach's cue on Day 2 - this replaces my earlier "palm faces down".)*
- Snap it back to guard along the same line, as fast as it went out.
- Right hand stays glued to your face the whole time.
- Looking along the fist is a beginner cue for aim. Against a real opponent, your eyes stay on them.
- Common mistake: dropping the left hand before punching or on the way back.

### 2 - Cross (rear straight, right hand)
**From class**
- Pivot the right (rear) foot as you punch.
- Fist ends in line with the top of the nose, eyes looking along the fist.
- Right shoulder rolls forward. Arm goes fully straight.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Pivot on the ball of the rear foot so the heel turns out, "squashing the bug". That turns the hips and is where the power comes from.
- Hips, then shoulder, then arm, in that order.
- The left hand returns to guard while the cross goes out.
- Don't lean your head past your front knee.
- Don't lock the elbow hard at full extension. A snapping lockout is one common source of shoulder and elbow pain.

### 3 - Lead hook (left)
**From class**
- Pivot the left foot so the body turns to the right.
- Arm bent at about 90 degrees.
- Fist, elbow and shoulder on the same plane (mặt phẳng).
- Fist, elbow and shoulder stay on one level - not above eye level. *(Confirmed with my coach on Day 2.)*

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The power comes from turning the lead foot, hip and shoulder together, not from swinging the arm.
- Elbow at shoulder height, forearm roughly level with the floor, so fist, elbow and shoulder sit on one level - matching your coach's cue.
- Keep the punch short and tight. Don't wind it back first.
- Right hand stays at your cheek. That side of your face is open while you hook.

### 4 - Rear hook (right)
**From class**
- Same as the lead hook, on the right side.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Pivot on the rear foot and turn the hips to the left.
- It travels further than the lead hook, so it's slower and easier to see coming. It's usually thrown after other punches, not on its own.
- Keep the left hand up and the chin tucked behind the right shoulder.

### 5 - Lead uppercut (left)
**From class**
- Hard one - my form is often wrong.
- The forearm (elbow to fist) stays straight up and down. My mistake: letting it tilt forward.
- At the top, the fist ends about one hand span (gang tay) from my face.
- Movement: bend the knees, dip the shoulder to load up (lấy đà), then punch from low to high.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The power comes from straightening your legs and turning your hips, not from swinging the arm up.
- Keep the dip small. A big drop shows the opponent what's coming.
- Don't drop your fist down to your waist before punching. Start from your guard.
- The fist ends around chin height, with the palm facing you.

### 6 - Rear uppercut (right)
**From class**
- Same as the lead uppercut, on the right side.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Turn the rear hip forward and drive up from the back leg.
- Left hand stays at your face.

### Punching basics (all punches)
**From class**
- Keep the fist relaxed, then squeeze it tight at the moment of impact.
- Breathe out through the mouth on every strike.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- A relaxed fist moves faster and tires you out less. Squeezing only on impact protects your hand.
- A sharp, short "tss" breath on each strike also tightens your core so the punch doesn't knock the wind out of you.
- Hit with the first two knuckles (index and middle), with the wrist straight - no bend up, down or sideways.

## Conditioning
- Plank, 2 minutes: fell 3 times.
- Crunches: 10 reps during class. At the end, 20 asked - managed 10.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Plank: body in a straight line from head to heels, glutes squeezed, no hips sagging or sticking up.
- To build up at home: 3 holds of 20-30 seconds with short rests, then add time each week.

## Body
- Knuckles, wrists, fingers and shoulder hurt after the session, from hitting hard.
- Wore hand wraps and gloves. No swelling - tired, not injured.
- My hands and wrists aren't used to this. Badminton doesn't need much strength from them.

**Common causes in beginners** *(added by Pi Liang - not a diagnosis)*
- Bent wrist or the wrong knuckles landing.
- Punching at full power before the technique is there. On day 1, speed and form matter more than power.
- Shoulder: locking the arm out hard on straight punches, or swinging the hooks from the arm.
- Tell your coach. See a doctor if there's swelling, sharp pain, numbness, or no improvement after a few days.

## New words
| What I said | Standard term | Note | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| right jab | **cross** (rear straight) | A jab is always the front hand. The rear straight is the cross. | Throw a jab, then follow it with a cross. |
| standing position | **stance** | Left foot forward = orthodox stance. | Get back into your stance after every punch. |
| spin the foot | **pivot** | Turn on the ball of the foot. | Pivot on your rear foot as you throw the cross. |
| forefoot | **ball of the foot** | The rounded, padded part of the sole just behind the toes, under the big-toe joint. Called a "ball" because of its rounded shape. | Stay on the balls of your feet so you can move quickly. |
| back heel slightly elevate | **rear heel slightly raised** | Or "up on the ball of the back foot". | Keep your rear heel slightly raised so you're ready to move. |
| shoulder tilt forward | **roll the shoulder** | Shoulder comes up to cover the chin. | Roll your shoulder up to protect your chin when you jab. |
| lấy đà | **load / load up** | Dip and coil before a power punch. | Don't load up too much - your opponent will see the uppercut coming. |
| gang tay | **hand span** | About the width of a spread hand. | At the top of the uppercut, your fist is about a hand span from your face. |
| mặt phẳng | **plane / level** | "Elbow level with the fist." | Keep your elbow level with your fist when you throw a hook. |
| keep a distance | **range / distance management** | | Stay at range so your opponent can't reach you. |
| both hands cover eyes | **guard / high guard** | | Keep your guard up, even when you're tired. |
| hide the chin | **tuck the chin** | | Tuck your chin so it's hidden behind your shoulder. |
| breathe out when hit | **exhale on the strike** | Hit = when you strike. "Get hit" = when you are struck. | Exhale on the strike so you don't run out of breath. |
