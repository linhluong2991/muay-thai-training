## Lesson

### 1-2-3 to the body + right kick
**From class**
- Also drilled the head version: 1-2-3 (jab-cross-lead hook), then right kick.
- Body version: the punches go to the body, not the face.
- Throw the 1-2-3 really fast. Jab, then the cross as I **drop my level** [knee down], keep the back straight, throw the **lead hook** straight away, then the right kick.
- I noticed that when I drop down, my face tilts forward - the opponent could hit me with their left elbow.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Drop by bending the knees, not the waist - the same cue as the body cross (Day 13) and the 2 - dip - 3 (Day 9). Your head should stay roughly above your front knee, never past it.
- Your elbow worry is right: in Muay Thai a head that leans forward is a target for elbows and knees, not just punches. Chin tucked, eyes up at their collar, rear glove glued to the face while the lead hand works.
- The lead hook to the body lands on their right side (the liver, against an orthodox opponent). Keep the elbow bent and the forearm roughly level, and turn the hips into it rather than reaching with the arm.
- Why the kick works here: body shots bring the opponent's hands and attention down, and the hook turns your hips left, which loads the right kick. Rise out of the low position straight into the kick - don't stand up first, then kick.
- Common beginner mistakes: pausing between the punches, staying low after the hook, and dropping the right hand when the kick swings.
- Home: Combo shadowboxing, round 6 (drills.md).

### 1-2 + right knee
**From class**
- Jab, cross, then **right knee** (rear straight knee).
- I usually forget to make the knee **like an arrow** (pointed), to **drive the hips forward**, and to **swing the right arm** as the knee comes up. After the coach reminded me, I did it right.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The cross turns your hips and shifts your weight forward, so the knee can come straight off it in the same rhythm - one-two-knee, no reset in between.
- Fold the leg tight (heel to thigh, toes pointed) so the point of the knee leads - the Day 7 cue "knee like an arrow".
- The knee travels forward into the target, not just up: rise onto the ball of the left foot and push the hips through.
- The right arm swings down and back past your hip as the knee drives - it balances you and adds hip drive. The left glove stays on your face.
- Knees work at close range, so in a real exchange the 1-2 often closes the distance or makes them cover up, then the knee lands.
- Home: Knee checklist drill (drills.md) - new today.

### 10 fast right kicks in a row
**From class**
- End of the set: 10 fast right kicks.
- From stance, step the left foot a little out to the left.
- On each kick, **pivot the standing foot** so the heel turns a bit toward the opponent [gót chân], and turn the hips over.
- **Bring the right leg back** [withdraw], but the left foot stays in kicking position - it doesn't go back to stance. Then kick again, 10 times in a row.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Leaving the standing foot turned out keeps the hips open, so each kick only needs the swing and the hip turn - that's what lets you kick fast and repeatedly. A full reset to stance would add a step to every kick.
- The kicking foot only touches down lightly behind you before it goes again - stay on the ball of the left foot, light and springy.
- Keep the arm swing on every kick, even when tired. Kicks without it turn into slow, pushing kicks with less hip.
- Common beginner mistakes: the standing foot slowly turns back flat (the kicks lose power), leaning back as you tire, and the left hand dropping.
- This drill is for rhythm and conditioning on the bag. In a fight you return to stance after a kick, as the coach taught in the double kick (Day 9).
- Home: Standing-foot pivot drill (drills.md).
