## Lesson

### Kicks on the heavy bag - four targets, both legs
**From class**
- 10 kicks at each target: **shin** (low kick), **thigh** (low kick), **body** (body kick), **head** (head kick).
- Both legs: right and left.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Same roundhouse kick at every height - only the target changes. Step out at an angle, rise onto the ball of the standing foot, turn the hips over, land with the shin.
- With the left leg, the switch has to happen before every kick - keep it small so it doesn't slow down each rep.
- Count the reps per leg: if the left side needs more effort to land the same kick, that's normal for now - it's where the extra home reps go.
- Home: Shin, not foot - bag distance drill, both legs.

### Station rotation - combos with the coach
**From class**
- Rotate through: one **plank**, one **crunch**, one round practising with the coach, then rest.
- 5 sets, a different combo each set. The combos mixed punches and kicks.
- **Left kick in a combo:** I have to hop to bring my right foot forward (**switch stance**) [jump right leg] before the kick, and this step doesn't feel smooth.
- **Right kick** in a combo still feels smooth.
- **Coach's correction:** after 1-2-3, step the **lead (left) foot** out to the left a little, then throw the right kick to the body.
- My left kick is weaker than my right kick.
- **Coach's correction:** use the hips - **turn the hips over** when you kick. I keep forgetting because I'm too focused on footwork and the target.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- **1-2-3 -> right body kick:** a classic combo. The lead hook turns your hips back to the left and loads the right side, so the kick comes straight off the hook. The small step out to the left with the lead foot opens the hips and gives the kick an angle, so the shin lands across the ribs instead of jamming into the bag.
- **The switch (left kick):** the hop should be part of the kick, not a separate step - switch and kick in one beat, without landing and stopping in between. Small hop, feet close to the floor, hands up.
- **Why the left kick is weaker:** usually a mix of three things - it's your non-dominant side, the switch takes away momentum if you pause after it, and the hips don't turn over as fully. Fixing the hip turn usually fixes most of the power.
- **Hip turn:** the power comes from the hips, not the leg. Think "belly button turns to the side" and the standing heel turns towards the target. If your attention is on the feet and the bag, pick one cue per round - this round only the hips.
- Some gyms teach a **step-up switch** instead of a hop (the rear foot steps up next to the lead foot, then the kick). Your coach taught the hop on Day 3, so stick with it. -> Ask the coach if a smaller switch would help in combos.
- Home: Hip-turn kick drill (drills.md).

### Power kicks and fast one-twos
**From class**
- 10 **rear roundhouse kicks** (right) with full power.
- 10 **switch kicks** (left) with full power.
- **Cross, then right kick** [right punch then right kick]: 10 with full power.
- Fast **1-2** (jab-cross): 50.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- **Cross -> right kick:** same-side combo. The cross turns your hips to the left; let them swing back and the right kick comes through. Bring the right hand back to guard before the kick lands, not after.
- Full power still means full technique: power comes from the hip turn and the pivot, not from swinging harder. If form breaks down, drop the power a little.
- **Fast one-twos:** speed comes from staying loose - the jab comes back as the cross goes out, shoulders relaxed, a short breath out on every punch. If the shoulders tighten, the punches slow down.
- Home: Combo shadowboxing, with the cross -> right kick added in round 3.
