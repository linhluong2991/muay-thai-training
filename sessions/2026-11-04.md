## Lesson

### Kicks on the heavy bag - low, body, high
**From class**
- Kicks at three heights: **low kick**, **body kick**, **high kick**.
- To kick with power: **lean the upper body** [nghiêng người], **turn the hips over** [xoay hông], and step the **standing foot** out, off the line of the bag [chân trụ bước ra khỏi bao] - then turn and kick.
- **My mistake:** I keep forgetting to turn my hips and lean properly.
- When I lean into the kick, I often **lose my balance**.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Stepping the standing foot out to the side opens the hips, so they have room to turn over. If the foot stays in line with the bag, the leg can only swing in flat.
- The lean is a counterweight: the kicking leg goes out one way, the upper body tilts the other way, so your weight stays over the standing foot. Lean to the side - never back (Day 3 cue).
- Balance usually goes when you lean without the pivot: the standing heel must turn towards the target as you lean, or your weight ends up behind your base.
- Lean only as much as the height needs: a little for the low kick, more for the body kick, most for the high kick.
- Home: Hip-turn kick drill and Wall pivot kick drill (drills.md).

### Plank relay - combos with the coach
**From class**

- Then 100 fast **1-2s**.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*

- On the 100 one-twos: a steady rhythm, loose shoulders, breathe out on every punch.

### Cutting the angle + right body kick
**From class**
- New today: **cutting the angle** [bẻ góc].
- Rise onto the **ball of the left foot** [nhón chân trái] and pivot by stepping the right foot across towards the left.
- After the pivot, throw a **right body kick**.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The pivot takes you off your opponent's centre line, so they have to turn to face you - and while they turn, your kick lands from a side that's hard to block.
- The left foot turns on the spot; only the right foot travels. Keep the turn small and quick - a big swing leaves you off balance.
- Hands stay up during the pivot - it's the moment you're most open.
- Kick straight off the pivot in one flow. If you pause to reset, the angle closes.
- Home: Punch and pivot (drills.md), round 4.

### Double right kick
**From class**
- Two right kicks in a row. After the first kick, the right foot comes back down **behind the left foot**; the left foot stays where it is. Kick again, then bring the right foot back behind the left foot again.
- **My mistake:** when the right foot comes back, it lands **level with the left foot** [ngang chân trái], so I have no power for the next kick. But when I bring it back behind the left foot, I lose my balance.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Landing level with the left foot is **landing square**: the hips already face forward, so there's no wind-up left for the second kick.
- Landing behind the left foot puts you back in stance, with the hips loaded again - that's why the coach wants it there.
- Losing balance on the way back usually comes from the upper body: if you're still leaning or turned when the foot lands, your weight falls with it. Let the leg swing back on the same path, and bring the shoulders and guard back to stance at the same moment as the foot.
- Land on the ball of the right foot, not flat, so you can push straight off it into the next kick.
- Slow first: kick, land in stance, pause one beat, kick. Take the pause out when the landing is steady.
- Home: Double kick reset drill (drills.md).

### Teep counter vs kicks
**From class**
- New today: when the opponent kicks to my **left side**, lift the **left leg** and **teep** them hard away, instead of **checking** [be].
- Same on the right: when they kick to my right side, teep with the right leg.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The teep gets there first: your straight leg is longer than their swinging kick, so you hit them while they're on one leg and push them off balance before the kick lands.
- Timing is everything - teep as their kick starts. Late, and you take the kick with your leg in the air. When in doubt, check.
- Push through with the hips - you're knocking them back, not tapping them. Aim where the coach said (sternum or upper stomach, below).
- Common mistake: leaning back to reach. Stay upright, guard up.
- The leg you'd check with is the same leg that teeps, so both moves start the same way - your opponent can't tell which is coming.
- Home: Fast check and foot shape drill (drills.md), round 3.

### Teep - targets and follow-ups
**From class**
- Teep hard to the **sternum** [xương ngực] or **upper stomach** [bụng trên].
- Kick, then teep. Or check, then teep.
- Check, then kick with the same leg.
- On knees and teeps, **drive the hips forward**.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The sternum and upper stomach are the middle of the body, so a hard teep there pushes the whole body back and can knock the wind out.
- After a check your leg is already up - a teep or kick with that same leg is the fastest answer you have, before your opponent resets.
- After a kick, the teep pushes the opponent back out of range before they can answer.
- Not noted whether the foot touches the floor between the check and the kick - worth asking the coach.
- Home: Fast check and foot shape drill (drills.md), round 3.

### Knee from the neck grab
**From class**
- On the right knee: the left hand stays tight to the head as the guard, the right hand grabs the opponent's neck [nắm cổ] and pulls them forward, and the knee comes up hard.
- Drive the hips forward.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- One hand on the neck is a **single collar tie**: the hand cups the back of the head (not the throat), with the forearm in front of their collarbone.
- The pull and the knee meet: the head comes towards you as the knee drives forward, so both forces add up.
- Pull forward, not just down: the opponent's head and upper body come towards you and fold over the knee, so the knee meets them as they come in.
- Keep the left glove on your own face the whole time - in the clinch the free hand is your only guard.
- Common mistake: pulling with the arm only. The pull and the hip drive happen together.

### 2 - dip - 3
**From class**
- **Cross (2)**, then drop the body down [hạ người xuống] and throw a **lead hook (3)**.
- First slowly, in 3 beats: 2 - dip - 3. Then faster.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The dip is a **level change**: bend the knees, not the waist, so your head drops off the line and a counter misses.
- The cross turns your hips one way, the hook turns them back - the same hip swing as 1-2-3, just lower.
- The right hand comes straight back to the face after the cross - keep the guard during the dip.
- Common mistakes: bending forward at the waist (the head goes forward, into punches) and dropping the hands.
- Home: Combo shadowboxing (drills.md), round 5.

### Slip and low kick vs the cross
**From class**
- A low kick to the opponent's **standing leg** [đá trụ] with the right leg.
- The left foot steps out to the left and the body leans left, so when the opponent throws a **cross** I slip it with the same movement. The right leg kicks the thigh of their standing leg.
- The left leg sinks low and digs in [hạ người thấp xuống, cắm xuống] for the kick.
- This position is hard and feels heavy - like a single-leg squat.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- One movement does two jobs: the step and lean take your head off the line of the cross, and they also open your hips for the kick.
- The kick lands while they're punching - their weight is on the front leg, so it can't lift to check.
- Sinking on the left leg is what makes it feel like a single-leg squat. It's mostly leg strength - it gets lighter as your legs get used to it.
- The right hand stays by your face as you lean - the lean moves your head, the hand still guards.
- Common mistake: leaning from the waist without stepping out. The foot moves first, the body follows.
- Home: Low base and balance builder (drills.md).

### 1-2, then step back
**From class**
- After the jab and cross, take one step back to get out of the way of the counter - like the clips I've seen online.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Hit and get out: right after a combo is when the opponent counters, so stepping back takes your head out of range.
- The rear foot steps back first, the lead foot follows - feet never cross, stance stays the same.
- Hands stay up as you step back - most counters land while the hands are coming home.
- Home: Punch and pivot (drills.md), round 3.

### Guard when defending
**From class**
- When defending, keep the gloves tight to the head with no gaps. Cover up over the eyes [ôm che mắt lại].
- **My mistake:** I often leave gaps.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Gloves on the forehead just above the eyes, elbows tight to the ribs - a punch that lands on the glove can't push it into your face.
- Gaps usually open when you peek out to see, or when the elbows drift out. Look over the gloves, not between them.
- Tired arms drop first - check the guard in the last round, not just the first.
- Home: Glued guard check (drills.md).

### Foot shape and shin contact
**From class**
- On a **check** [be], **flex the foot** [cong bàn chân].
- On a **knee**, **point the foot** [duỗi bàn chân]. On a **kick**, point the foot too.
- I often forget.
- Kick precisely with the **shin** - landing with the wrong part hurts the foot, toes and ankle.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Flexed foot on the check: it tightens the muscles around the shin and ankle, so the shin is solid when the kick lands on it.
- Pointed foot on the kick: the foot stays in line with the shin, so the toes don't catch the target.
- Pointed foot on the knee: it tightens the leg so the knee stays the point of the strike.
- Easy rule: defend = flex, attack = point.
- Home: Fast check and foot shape drill (drills.md); Shin, not foot - bag distance drill.

|---|---|---|---|
| bẻ góc | **cutting the angle** / **angle step** | Pivoting off the opponent's centre line to attack from the side. | Cut the angle, then kick him while he turns. |

| nắm cổ đối phương | **single collar tie** | One hand behind the opponent's head. Both hands is the **plum** (Thai clinch). | Grab the collar tie and drive the knee. |
| hạ người xuống | **level change** / **dip** | Bend the knees to drop the head - not the waist. | Two, dip, then rip the hook. |
