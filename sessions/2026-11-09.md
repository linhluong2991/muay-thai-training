## Lesson

### Kicks - low, body, high
**From class**
- 15 kicks at each height - **low kick**, **body kick** [mid], **high kick** - with the left leg and the right leg.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Same kick at every height: step the standing foot out off the line, rise onto the ball of the foot and pivot, lean to the side, turn the hips over, land with the shin.
- 15 per height is 90 kicks in all - the last ones are where the hip turn and the pivot start to slip. Keep the kicks full even if they get slower.
- Home: Hip-turn kick drill and Standing-foot pivot drill (drills.md).

### Sparring
**From class**
- Sparring with other people.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- When you punch, the other hand stays on your face, and the shoulder of the punching arm rises to cover the chin: lead shoulder on the jab, rear shoulder on the cross. That's the cover you felt was missing.
- Feeling right with the coach and open in sparring is normal: on pads you know what's coming; in sparring you're thinking about attack, defence and movement at once, so the guard is the first thing to go.
- Beginners often lean the head forward to reach a punch - the face then sits right on the line of the counter. Step in to punch rather than lean.
- A simple sparring plan: one combo, hands home, then step out or cut the angle. Attack - cover - out. Don't stay in front of your partner after you punch.
- Pick one thing per round (for example: "the other hand stays on my face"), not everything at once.
- Home: Cover while you punch (new), Punch and pivot, Glued guard check (drills.md).

### Plank relay - combos with the coach
**From class**
- 7 people in the team today. One person does the combo with the coach while everyone else holds a **plank**, then the next person goes. 5 sets.
- Then 50 fast **1-2s** (jab-cross), then 5 x 1-2 with **full power**.
- On the fast 1-2s, my right glove going out and my left glove coming back seem to **collide** [tangle] halfway.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Each punch travels on its own line: the jab goes out and back along the same path to the left cheek, the cross leaves from the right cheek. If the lines curve, the gloves meet in the middle.
- Common causes of the gloves touching: the elbow flaring out (makes the path curve - see the Elbow wall drill), the jab coming back low or late, or the cross starting before the jab has started to return.
- Speed comes from staying loose, not from rushing. Slow it down to the speed where the gloves never touch, then build up.
- Fast sets train speed; full-power sets train putting the whole body in: pivot the rear foot and turn the hips on the cross, breathe out sharply on every punch.
- Home: Cover while you punch (drills.md), round 3.

### Crunch relay
**From class**
- Each person counts from 1 to 10 in turn while everyone does crunches - about 80 crunches in total, with the coach joining in.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Breathe out on the way up, chin tucked, eyes up - pull with the abs, not the neck.
- On a long count, keep every rep small and clean rather than rushing half reps.

## Body
- Calluses starting to form [bị chai] on the knuckles of my right index and middle fingers. The middle knuckle is clearly visible; the index is less clear.
- My left hand doesn't have them.

**Common causes in beginners** *(added by Pi Liang - not a diagnosis)*
- Calluses come from repeated rubbing and pressure, inside the wraps and gloves - lots of punching volume, like today's 50 fast and 5 full-power 1-2s.
- The right is your rear hand: the cross carries the hip turn and usually lands harder than the jab, so the right knuckles take more.
- Index and middle knuckles are the ones you're meant to land with, so the spot shows you're landing in the right place.
- Wraps that cover the knuckles well keep the skin from tearing or blistering while it toughens. If the skin cracks, swells or hurts to make a fist, check with the coach or a doctor.
