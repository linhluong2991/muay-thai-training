## Lesson

### Kicks on the heavy bag - four targets
**From class**
- **Low kick** to the shin: 15.
- **Low kick** to the thigh: 15.
- **Body kick** (middle kick): 15.
- **Head kick** (high kick): 15.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Same roundhouse kick at four heights. Only the target changes: the mechanics stay the same - step out at an angle, rise onto the ball of the standing foot and pivot, turn the hips over, land with the shin.
- **Thigh kick:** aim the lower shin at the outside of the thigh, just above the knee. Kick slightly downward through the leg rather than flat.
- **Low kick at shin height:** the lowest target. Bend the standing leg a little more so you don't have to lean down to reach it - the back stays straight.
- Land every kick with the shin, not the top of the foot (Day 4 blisters). If the foot keeps landing first, step a little closer.
- Home: the Shin, not foot - bag distance drill, at low, thigh and body height.

### Shoulder-and-knee tag game
**From class**
- Partner game: each pair tries to **tag** [tap] the other's shoulder and knee. Whoever gets tagged does a push-up. For me, a squat instead.
- 2-minute rounds, then rotate partners. Class: 4 teammates + me + the coach, so 5 partners per rotation. I did 3 rotations (2 min x 5 partners x 3).
- I was on the balls of my feet and moving quite freely.
- I tagged my teammates' shoulders and knees, including the coach's.
- Later, when I got too tired, they tagged me.
- **My mistake:** I didn't move left or right - I kept **circling**, and that drained my stamina.
- The coach and my teammates **switched stance** [switched their standing leg / base leg] during the game - changing which foot is in front.
- Eyes on the opponent's **collarbone**.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The game trains distance, timing and reactions: get in, tag, get out before they tag back - the same rhythm as attacking in sparring.
- **Circling vs moving off-line:** circling all the time keeps you at the same distance and costs a lot of energy. Mix it up: a short side-step to cut an angle, a step in to tag, a step back out. Small steps, then stop and reset your stance.
- To tag the knee you have to drop level: bend your knees to go down, don't bend forward at the waist - bending forward leaves your own shoulders and head open.
- **Switching stance** changes your angle and which side is closer to the opponent. It's normal for experienced fighters; for now, keep your usual stance and footwork clean first. -> Ask the coach when you should start switching.
- **Eyes on the collarbone:** from there you can see the shoulders (punches) and, at the edge of your vision, the hips and legs (kicks), without chasing the hands with your eyes.
- Home: Side-step box drill (drills.md).

### Sparring - punches and kicks only
**From class**
- Free sparring in pairs, punches and kicks only (kickboxing style).
- 2-minute rounds, rotating partners: 2 min x 5 partners x 2 rotations.
- I could land punches and kicks - on Day 2 I could barely hit anyone.
- I need smoother footwork - I keep circling.
- I need to combine the moves I've learned into **combinations**, not throw them one at a time.
- I need to **evade** [dodge], not just take hits.
- I don't always remember to **check** [be] the kick. I can check when we're more than an arm's length apart - I can see their hip and the kick coming. But against a taller partner, or when they come in closer than an arm's length, I get kicked easily.
- Eyes on the opponent's collarbone (sets 2 and 3).
- One partner I could never hit in the face: he dodged fast by **leaning back** [moving his head backward] out of range of the punch.
- My shoulders are too stiff when I defend - raising my hands to cover my face - so I get tired easily.
- **A teammate's tip:** when he kicks and I focus on blocking the kick, I forget to cover my face - then he punches me in the face. He's right. I tried the same move on him.
- What I learned: we're all beginners, so we all make mistakes - like doing one move and forgetting to cover another part. We also tend to throw single moves instead of combinations.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- **Check with the guard up:** when one leg rises to check, both hands stay home - the same-side elbow drops to meet the knee, the other glove stays on your cheek. Your teammate found the classic set-up: kick to make you look down, then punch the open face.
- His move works in reverse too: **punches to set up kicks, kicks to set up punches.** That's why combinations beat single strikes - a single shot is easy to read, the second one lands while they're reacting to the first.
- **Taller partner or close range:** a taller partner's kick reaches you from further away, so you're often inside their range before you feel it. Either stay out of range, or step in fully so the kick has no room - standing half-in is where you get kicked. At close range the hip is hard to see, so keep the lead leg light and ready to check.
- **Evading:** the simplest way is footwork - step back or angle off. Your partner used the **lean back** (sway back): the head moves back just out of reach. Against it, double the jab and step in with the second one, or go to the body or legs - a leaning fighter's weight is on the back foot and the body stays close. Head movement (slipping, ducking) usually comes later. -> Ask the coach what to start with.
- **Relaxed guard:** the gloves rest lightly on the face, shoulders down and away from the ears, breathing out. Tighten only at the moment a strike lands, then let go again. A guard held tight for 2 minutes burns the shoulders before the legs.
- Being tired makes all of this worse - the hands drop and the feet stop. Good footwork (fewer, smaller steps) saves energy for the late rounds.
- Home: Check and cover drill (drills.md), plus Combo shadowboxing.
