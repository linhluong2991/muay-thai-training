## Lesson

### Slip right and counter cross
**From class**
- After I throw the **1-2**, my right foot is already on the ball of the foot from the cross. I keep that and move the right foot a little to the right.
- My body tilts to the right to **slip** [dodge] the opponent's **jab**: right shoulder a bit lower than the left, hips turn a little to the right, both knees bent a little. The left hand stays on my face.
- Then, using the momentum from the right foot and the hip turn together, I throw the **cross** back at the opponent. That completes the move.
- We practised the move with team members.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Your description matches the standard move: slipping to the outside of the jab, then countering with the cross. The opponent's jab goes past your left ear, and you end up outside their lead arm, where their right hand can't reach you easily.
- The slip loads the punch: turning the hips right and sinking onto the right leg winds you up like a spring, so the cross comes off that loaded leg and hip with more power than a cross from standing still.
- Move the head just enough - a few centimetres off the line of the jab is enough. Common beginner mistakes: leaning so far the balance goes, bending at the waist instead of the knees, dropping the eyes to the floor, or letting the left hand fall while slipping.
- Keep the eyes on the opponent the whole time - you slip what you see.
- Home: shadowbox it in 3 beats - 1-2, slip right, 2 - slowly in front of a mirror, checking that your head moves off your centre line and your left glove stays on your face. Then join the beats together.

### Combos
**From class**
- 1-2-1-2
- 1-2-3-2
- 5-6-3-4
- 1-6-3-2
- The last punch of the combo is the strongest one.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- In words: 1-2-3-2 = jab, cross, lead hook, cross. 5-6-3-4 = lead uppercut, rear uppercut, lead hook, rear hook. 1-6-3-2 = jab, rear uppercut, lead hook, cross.
- The early punches set up the last one: they make the opponent cover up or react, and the last punch lands with the whole body behind it.
- Each punch turns the hips the opposite way to the one before (left side, right side, left side...). Let the turn from one punch start the next - that's what makes the last one heavy.
- 5-6 and 3-4 are close-range punches: stay close and keep the elbows in, don't reach.
- After the last punch, hands back to the face, then step out or cut the angle.
- Home: Combo shadowboxing (drills.md) with these four, 1 round each.

## Body
- My left pinky feels tired [mỏi]. I think when I punch with my left hand, the impact lands on the pinky side, not on the index and middle knuckles.

**Common causes in beginners** *(added by Pi Liang - not a diagnosis)*
- Your guess fits Day 11: calluses formed on the right index and middle knuckles but not on the left. That suggests the left hand is landing on a different part of the fist.
- The wrist bends on impact: if the wrist bends down or out, the fist tilts and the outer knuckles (ring and pinky) take the hit. The back of the hand should stay flat in line with the forearm.
- Rolling the fist too far on the jab or the lead hook: a little tilt is fine, but too much brings the pinky knuckles forward. Your lead hand throws the jab, the lead hook and the lead uppercut, so it gets this a lot.
- The pinky doesn't close tight: if the pinky isn't curled in on impact, the fist doesn't have a solid shape and the pinky takes the strain. Squeezing the fist the whole time (instead of only on impact) also tires the small fingers.
- Wraps that are loose around the knuckles or the wrist give less support.
- Check: throw slow jabs at your open palm or a wall and look at which knuckles touch first. If the pinky gets sore, swells or hurts to make a fist, check with the coach or a doctor.

## New words
