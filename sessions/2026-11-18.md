## Lesson

### Roundhouse kicks on the bag - coach's corrections
**From class**
- Bag kicks at low, mid and high height, 15 each leg.
- Coach's feedback: **turn the hips** over, **point the foot** [duỗi mu bàn chân] when the shin lands, and **keep the back straight**.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The three cues work together: the hip turn carries the shin through the target, the pointed foot locks the ankle so the shin lands (not the toes or the top of the foot), and a straight back keeps your weight over the standing foot so the hips can turn all the way.
- "Back straight" means a long spine, not standing upright: you still tilt the upper body away from the kick as a counterweight (Day 9), but in one line from head to hips - no hunching or folding at the waist to reach the bag.
- Common beginner mistakes: kicking with the leg only while the hips stay square, bending at the waist to reach, and the foot going floppy on impact.
- While the movement is still new, a lower, slower kick with all three cues beats a high kick with none of them.
- Home: Hip-turn kick drill (drills.md) - today's three cues added.

### Footwork rhythm into the step jab
**From class**
- Both feet move **in rhythm**: left, right, left, right.
- Then **jab** with the left foot **stepping forward** a little (**step jab**).

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The rhythm is a light, small weight shift from foot to foot on the balls of the feet, staying in stance - not running on the spot. Relaxed, so you can go at any moment.
- The jab goes out on a beat: the left foot and the fist land together (the Day 9 cue - not step, then jab). The rear foot slides up after, so the stance keeps its width.
- Why it helps: the rhythm keeps you loose and hides the moment you attack. A jab from a still, flat-footed stance is easier to read.
- Common beginner mistakes: bouncing too high (slow and tiring), the stance narrowing or the feet crossing, the right hand drifting down during the rhythm.
- Home: Step-and-jab timing (drills.md) - start each rep from the left-right rhythm.

### Check, then knee or kick
**From class**
- When the opponent throws a **right kick**, **check** [be] it with my left leg. As the left leg comes down, **step it down forward** - that loads the **right knee**.
- What I remember: check with the left leg, then **right kick**; or check with the left leg, then **left kick** (same leg). The same on the right side.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Against an orthodox opponent, their right kick comes to your left side, so the left leg checks - same side as the kick (Day 3).
- Landing the left foot forward does two jobs: it closes the distance to knee range, and it puts your weight on the front leg with the right hip back and loaded - the right knee drives straight off it.
- Check -> right kick: the checking leg lands and becomes your standing leg; rise onto the ball of the left foot and kick while they're still recovering from their own kick.
- Check -> same-leg kick: the fastest answer, because the leg is already up (Day 9).
- Keep both hands up through the whole chain - check, land, strike - or they kick to make you check and then punch (Check and cover drill).
- Common beginner mistakes: putting the checking foot back where it was (the knee falls short), dropping the hands on the check, and a pause between the check and the strike.
- Home: Fast check and foot shape drill (drills.md) - new round: check left, land forward, right knee.

### 1-2, grab the neck, right knee
**From class**
- Punch 1-2, then use the right hand to **grab the opponent's neck**, **pull their head** toward me, and throw the **right knee**.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- This is the single collar tie + rear knee (Day 9) with a 1-2 in front: the punches close the distance and make them cover up, then the right hand goes straight from the cross to the back of their neck.
- Grab high on the back of the head, not the throat, and pull the head down and toward you - their head comes down as your knee comes up.
- Pull and knee together. The right arm is busy pulling, so the pull takes the place of the arm swing.
- The left glove stays glued to your face - at this range their elbows and knees land too.
- Common beginner mistakes: grabbing without pulling (they stay tall and the knee hits nothing), a pause between the cross and the grab, and the left hand dropping.
- Home: Knee checklist drill, round 6 (drills.md).

### Teep in the middle of their kick
**From class**
- The opponent throws a right roundhouse kick. In the middle of the kick, **teep** with my left leg.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Their right kick comes to your left side, so the left (lead) teep is the closest answer - the same rule as the teep counter on Day 9.
- Mid-kick they're on one leg and turning, so a teep then knocks them off balance and stops the kick before it lands with full power.
- Same teep mechanics: ball of the foot to the sternum or upper stomach, push the hips forward, left arm swings, right glove on the face.
- Common beginner mistakes: teeping too late (the kick lands first), a short teep that only touches them, and leaning back instead of pushing the hips in.
- Home: Fast check and foot shape drill, round 3 (drills.md) - do the left teep without the check first.

### Catch the right kick, throw the leg, then counter
**From class**
- The opponent throws a right kick. I **catch** it with my left hand, move backward, **throw their leg** away with my left hand, then throw the **right hook** or the **right kick**.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Same rule as the Day 4 coach's tip: catch with the left hand, step back with the left leg. The step back drags them forward onto their standing leg.
- Trap the leg against your ribs, close to the body - a catch far out in front lets them pull the leg back.
- Throwing the leg away lands them off balance and turned, and your right side is already loaded - so the right hook or right kick comes straight after, before they plant their feet.
- Keep the right glove on your face while you catch; free hands are for defending until the throw.
- Common beginner mistakes: catching with the arm out, a pause after the throw, and the counter coming after they've already reset.
- Home: Morning review flow, block 5 (drills.md) - add "catch with the left hand, step back, throw, right kick".

### Low kick with the coach - eyes up
**From class**
- Low kick with the coach: eyes on the opponent's **eyes** or **collarbone**, and the right leg kicks their **thigh**.
- I found this difficult because I keep looking at the pad on the coach's thigh to make sure I kick correctly.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Looking down tells them where the kick is going, drops your head and guard, and means you can't see their punches coming.
- Eyes on the collarbone is the same view as the base of the neck (Day 1): your side vision still sees the thigh, so you don't need to look at it.
- Accuracy comes from distance, not from looking: find the range where the lower shin lands, then trust it.
- Common beginner mistakes: eyes on the target, the head dropping with the eyes, and lunging in from too far away.
- Home: Eyes-up low kick drill (drills.md) - new today.

### Teeps and knees - arm swing
**From class**
- On **teeps** and **knees** I keep forgetting to **swing the arm**.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Which arm: lead teep and switch knee -> the left arm swings; rear teep and rear knee -> the right arm swings. The other glove stays on the face.
- The swing is a counterbalance: as the hips drive forward, the arm goes down and back past the hip, keeping you upright and adding hip drive.
- Common beginner mistakes: both hands dropping (Day 7), swinging the wrong arm, and a big wild swing that opens the face.
- Home: Knee checklist drill (drills.md) - now with teeps too.
