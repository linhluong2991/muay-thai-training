## Lesson

### Roundhouse kick - three targets
**From class**
- Three targets for the roundhouse kick: **calf kick** (below the knee), **body kick**, and **head kick**.
- Practised kicks on the bag and with the coach.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Same kick, three heights. The calf kick is the lowest and safest to throw, the head kick needs the most flexibility and balance, so it's normal for it to come last.
- **Calf kick:** lands on the back or side of the opponent's calf, below the knee. It's quick and low-risk, and it slows the opponent's footwork down over a fight.
- **Body kick:** aims at the ribs, under the arm. The most common kick in Muay Thai.
- **Head kick:** same mechanics, but you turn the hip over even more and lean a little further to the side. Height comes from the hip turn and the standing foot pivot, not from swinging the leg harder.
- Common mistake at every height: kicking with the foot instead of the shin. The foot is small and full of bones; the shin is the weapon.

### Rear roundhouse kick (right leg)
**From class**
- **Step out** with the lead (left) foot at an angle, a little outside the opponent's right side.
- Kick with the **shin** [ống chân], with the **foot pointed** [duỗi mu bàn chân].
- **Turn the hips over** [xoay hông].
- Right hand at shoulder height [tay phải ngang vai].
- Back straight, or leaning slightly to the right. **Don't lean back** [không nghiêng lưng ra đằng sau].

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The step out at an angle opens your hips and puts you off the opponent's centre line, so the kick has room to swing through.
- **Pivot on the ball of the standing (left) foot** so the heel turns towards the target. If the standing foot stays flat and facing forward, the hips can't turn and you get pulled off balance - this is the most common reason beginners wobble.
- Land the kick with the lower part of the shin, a hand's width above the ankle.
- The arm on the kicking side swings for counterbalance. Many gyms swing it down past the hip; your coach teaches it at shoulder height - follow the coach.
- Keep the lead hand up guarding your face the whole time.
- After the kick, bring the leg back to stance (or let it swing through on the bag) and return to guard straight away.

### Lead roundhouse kick (left leg) - switch kick
**From class**
- Small jump to **switch stance** [jump change feet]: the right foot ends up forward.
- Then turn the hip and kick with the left leg, the same way as the right kick.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- This is called a **switch kick**. The switch turns your lead leg into a rear leg, so it gets the same distance and hip turn as the right kick.
- Keep the switch small and fast - a little hop, feet staying close to the floor. A big jump gives the kick away and makes balance harder.
- Hands stay up during the switch; that's the moment beginners drop their guard.
- After switching, it's the same checklist as the right kick: pivot the standing foot, turn the hips over, kick with the shin.

### Checking kicks (shin block)
**From class**
- Block a kick with the leg, on both sides [block with 2 legs].

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Blocking a kick with your shin is called **checking** (or a **shin block**). Lift the knee and turn it slightly out so the kick lands on your shin, not on your thigh or knee.
- Lift the leg on the same side the kick comes from: a kick to your left side gets a left check, a kick to your right gets a right check.
- Keep the guard up while checking - a kick is often followed by a punch.
- Standing on one leg is a balance skill too: stand tall, don't lean back, and put the foot down quickly in stance.
- -> **Ask the coach** if "block with 2 legs" meant checking with either leg, or something else (for example, a double block with both legs).

### Tire circuit with padwork
**From class**
- Jump onto the truck tire, alternating left and right legs. (I don't know the name.)
- Then padwork with the coach: combos like 1-2-1-2 and 1-2-3, plus one more I forgot.
- Then lift the truck tire above my head and walk around with it.
- Repeat 5 times.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- The tire jumps build quick, springy feet and leg endurance; the overhead tire walk builds shoulders, core and grip, the muscles that keep your guard up late in a fight.
- **1-2-1-2:** two one-twos back to back. Keep each jab sharp even when tired - the second jab is where beginners get lazy.
- **1-2-3:** the cross turns your hips, the hook turns them back. No pause between the punches.
- Overhead walk: arms locked, ribs down, abs tight so the lower back doesn't arch. Short, steady steps.
- Padwork when you're tired is training too: keep the guard up and breathe out on every punch.

### Defence against the 6 punches
**From class**
- Defence against the punches in pairs: 1 and 2, 3 and 4, 5 and 6.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Each pair needs a different defence because the punches come from a different direction:
  - **1-2 (straights):** come straight down the middle. Common answers: **parry** (a small tap that knocks the punch aside), **catch** it with the palm, or **slip** the head off the line.
  - **3-4 (hooks):** come from the side. Common answer: **cover** - raise the forearm to the side of the head, elbow up, glove tight against the head.
  - **5-6 (uppercuts):** come from below. Common answer: tuck the chin and close the forearms in front of the body and face, or catch the punch with the palm.
- Small, tight movements. A big reach for the punch leaves a gap for the next one.

### Sparring
**From class**
- Sparring with the coach and 4 other partners.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- Use sparring to test one thing at a time, for example: cover the hooks, or throw the 1-2 as one movement.
- After kicks class, a simple plan: block (check) one kick, then answer with a 1-2.

### Stretching
**From class**
- Stretching before the end of the session.

**How it works** *(added by Pi Liang)*
- For kicks, the most useful stretches are the hips and hamstrings: hip flexor lunge, butterfly stretch, and a standing hamstring stretch. Hold each for about 30 seconds, no bouncing.
- Better hip mobility makes the hip turn easier, which helps balance on the kick.
